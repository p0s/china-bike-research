## Compare finished-bike cost

A frameset price excludes the drivetrain, brakes, crank, cassette, chain, bottom bracket, rotors, wheels, tires, cockpit, saddle, tape, small parts, tools, and assembly. Manufacturers buy those parts at volume prices. That is why a ¥5,191 complete bike can cost less than a direct frame plus the same category of electronic drivetrain bought at retail.

Use the build calculator on every frameset page. It applies one reusable parts profile so assumptions remain consistent.

## A frameset is rational when it buys something specific

Choose a frameset for geometry that fits, 45–57 mm clearance, UDH, BSA/T47, internal storage, rack or fender mounts, exact cockpit dimensions, paint, known frame weight, or a stronger manufacturing/support proposition.

Do not choose it merely because building sounds cheaper.

## Include fit-change costs

An integrated cockpit may be included, but changing stem length or bar width can cost hundreds or thousands of RMB. A complete bike with the wrong cockpit can erase its headline value. A conventional stem/bar may be heavier or less sleek but easier to fit and replace.

## Include resale and service

A recognized complete SKU can be easier to describe and resell. A custom build may have better parts but a smaller buyer pool. Proprietary headset parts, seatposts, hangers, storage doors, and cockpits need a replacement plan regardless of whether you buy complete or build.

## A useful decision rule

Buy the complete when it fits, meets the actual clearance need, and gives a meaningfully lower finished price. Build from a frame when the frame itself solves a real requirement that the value complete does not.
