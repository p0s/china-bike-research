## Four different numbers are often called “clearance”

1. **Stock nominal size** — the label printed on the supplied tire.
2. **Manufacturer maximum** — the largest nominal size the brand says may fit.
3. **Measured tire width** — the actual casing width on a stated rim at a stated pressure.
4. **Remaining frame clearance** — the minimum gap from the inflated tire to the fork, stays, seat tube, and other constraints.

Only the last two describe the actual setup.

## A 40C tire is not necessarily 40 mm

Casing design, rim internal width, pressure, production tolerance, and measurement time all matter. A nominal 40C tire may measure several millimetres wider. A seller saying “supports 40C” is therefore incomplete unless the rim and tire are identified and the remaining space is shown.

## Use the tightest point

Measure both sides of the fork, chainstays, seatstays, and the area near the seat tube. On asymmetric frames, front and rear maxima differ. A frame advertised as 58 mm front / 53 mm rear is effectively a 53 mm matched-tire platform unless you intend to run different sizes.

## Leave a use-dependent margin

There is no single universal safe number. As a practical buyer screen, dry-road use needs less margin than gravel with small stones, and mud needs substantially more. Wheels flex, debris adheres, and a slightly out-of-true wheel reduces the available gap.

The guide therefore labels evidence rather than pretending a nominal maximum is a guaranteed fit. “Official 50C” is stronger than “40C stock, maximum unknown,” but it remains a claim until a specific tire/rim combination is measured.

## Ask for useful photos and measurements

Request the installed tire model, rim internal width, measured inflated width, and the smallest gap at the fork crown, fork legs, chainstays, seatstays, and seat tube. A ruler held at a flattering angle is not precise; calipers and clear perpendicular photos are better.

## Do not infer between generations

The same marketing name may cover different molds or model years. Never transfer a clearance figure from a flat-bar version, previous generation, or another configuration without verifying that the frame platform is identical.
